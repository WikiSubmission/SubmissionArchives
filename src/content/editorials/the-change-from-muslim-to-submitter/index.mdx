<Lead>
In August 1989 the monthly bulletin of Masjid Tucson was called *Muslim Perspective*. In September it was called *Submission Perspective*. By January 1990 it was called *Submitters Perspective*, and it kept that name. The change is not an inference drawn from the theology; it is a physical fact of the printed run, visible across sixty-four consecutive issues held in the archive, and the issue that made the first change opens with an editorial headed "Why the name change?"
</Lead>

This editorial reconstructs that decision from the documents that recorded it as it happened: the bulletin itself, a study session in which the argument was worked out aloud, and an FBI file that noted the rename from the outside without any interest in what it meant.

The run held in the archive covers February 1985 to March 1990, sixty-four issues in all.<Ref id="1" /> The title sequence within it is exact:

| Issues | Masthead |
|---|---|
| February 1985 – August 1989 | **Muslim Perspective** |
| September 1989 – December 1989 | **Submission Perspective** |
| January 1990 – March 1990 | **Submitters Perspective** |

The first change lands on issue **No. 57**. The second lands on No. 61, the January 1990 issue, which was the last one Dr. Rashad Khalifa edited before he was murdered on the 31st of that month. Three further issues carried the new masthead without him.

## 2. "Why the Name Change?"

The September 1989 issue does not leave the reasoning to be reconstructed. It states it on page one, under that heading, over the editor's name.<Ref id="2" />

<PullQuote attribution="Submission Perspective, September 1989, No. 57, editorial">
The name of this bulletin is now SUBMISSION PERSPECTIVE instead of MUSLIM PERSPECTIVE. This marks the advent of momentous changes and ushers in a new era of unification. Henceforth, all believers who devote their worship to the One True God ALONE will join forces under the banner of "One True Religion."
</PullQuote>

The argument that follows is linguistic before it is theological, and it rests on a claim about scripture that the reader is invited to check verse by verse:

<PullQuote attribution="Submission Perspective, September 1989, No. 57, editorial">
The words "Muslim" and "Islam" are NOT names; they are Arabic words meaning "Submitter" and "Submission," respectively. When Noah, Abraham, Solomon, and even the jinns talked about "Islam" and "Muslim" they did not speak in Arabic; they used their own languages. Please read these verses: 2:131; 7:126; 10:72; 22:78; 27:31,42; 28:53; 72:14. When Jesus' disciples stated that they were "Muslims," they did not say it in Arabic (3:52).
</PullQuote>

The editorial then supplies the analogy that makes the point difficult to argue with, because it runs in the opposite direction:

<PullQuote attribution="Submission Perspective, September 1989, No. 57, editorial">
A Christian Arab is called "Nusraany" in Arabic. Yet, we do not use this Arabic word when we speak English. It has been Satan's plan to transform these "descriptions" into "names," then to give "Islam" and the "Muslims" a bad name. We have now mobilized to pull the rug from under Satan's feet.
</PullQuote>

An English speaker does not call an Arab Christian a *Nusraany*; the word is translated as a matter of course. The editorial's charge is that one word, and only one, was left untranslated, and that the exception did the damage.

## 3. The Argument Worked Out Aloud

The archive also holds the reasoning being arrived at rather than announced. In a recorded study session in Tucson, a member of the circle raised the practical case first, from experience in the field:<Ref id="3" />

<PullQuote attribution="Study session, Tucson, recorded">
"Would you submit? Are you a submitter to God? Do you believe in God? Do you submit to Him?" This is a door opener. Hardly you can find anyone who can dare saying "I do not," unless he says "I'm an atheist and I don't believe in God." So, the word "Muslim" may introduce an element of prejudice.
</PullQuote>

Khalifa's answer accepted the point and put a history behind it. He noted that the mathematical discovery had first been published under the title *The Perpetual Miracle of Muhammad*, a framing calculated to be maximally acceptable to a traditional audience, and had been rejected anyway. He then named what he thought had happened to the word itself:

<PullQuote attribution="Dr. Rashad Khalifa, study session, Tucson">
Islam was given a bad name, thanks to Khomeini and Gaddafi and Islamic revolutionaries. In the new, in the final and official translation that I'm doing now [...] it says "the only religion approved by God is Submission." And "Muslim" will be changed to "Submitter." The "Muslim Perspective" has been changed to "Submitters Perspective."
</PullQuote>

That is a decision being reported in the past tense about the bulletin and in the future tense about the translation, which places the session after the masthead had already changed and while the 1989 translation was still in production.

## 4. The Change Seen From Outside

One further witness is worth having, because it had no stake in any of this. When FBI Los Angeles catalogued Khalifa's publications in a teletype five days after his murder, the agent recorded the rename as a plain administrative fact:<Ref id="4" />

<PullQuote attribution="FBI teletype FBILA 0005, 5 February 1990">
TWO PUBLICATIONS WERE IDENTIFIED [...] AS RELIGIOUS JOURNALS THAT RASHAD DISTRIBUTED. THEY WERE IDENTIFIED AS THE "MOSLEM PERSPECTIVE" WHICH WAS SUBSEQUENTLY RENAMED THE "SUBMITTERS PERSPECTIVE."

</PullQuote>

Nobody at the Bureau cared what the change meant. That is precisely what makes the entry useful: it confirms the fact of the rename from a source with no interest in the theology behind it.

## 5. What the Verses Say

The editorial's claim is that the Quran uses these words as descriptions rather than as the proper name of a seventh-century community. The verses it cites can be read directly.

<Verse chapter={3} verses="19">
The only religion approved by GOD is "Submission." Ironically, those who have received the scripture are the ones who dispute this fact, despite the knowledge they have received, due to jealousy. For such rejectors of GOD's revelations, GOD is most strict in reckoning.
</Verse>

The naming is attributed not to Arabia but to Abraham, who preceded the Torah, the Gospel and the Quran alike:

<Verse chapter={22} verses="78">
You shall strive for the cause of GOD as you should strive for His cause. He has chosen you and has placed no hardship on you in practicing your religion, the religion of your father Abraham. He is the one who named you "Submitters" originally.
</Verse>

And the category, once translated, turns out to be defined by three conditions rather than by inheritance or membership:

<Verse chapter={2} verses="62">
Surely, those who believe, those who are Jewish, the Christians, and the converts; anyone who (1) believes in GOD, and (2) believes in the Last Day, and (3) leads a righteous life, will receive their recompense from their Lord. They have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve.
</Verse>

## 6. A Note on the Number

Readers of this archive will notice that the first name change fell on issue No. 57, and that 57 = 19 × 3. The observation is recorded here because it is checkable: the bulletin numbered its issues sequentially and without a break, June 1989 is No. 54, and the count runs 55, 56, 57 into the change.

It is also worth stating what it is not. Issue numbers advance one at a time, so exactly one issue in every nineteen carries a multiple of nineteen, and something had to happen in that issue. The archive publishes the number because a reader would find it anyway, and publishes the caveat in the same breath because the alternative is to let arithmetic do work that the September 1989 editorial already does better on its own terms.

## 7. Archival Significance

The value of this record is that the change is documented rather than argued. A reader who wants to know whether the terminology was a later invention of the movement can settle it in an afternoon: the mastheads are dated, the issue numbers are sequential, the editorial announcing the change is on page one of the issue where the change occurs, and an indifferent third party noted the same fact from outside four months later.
