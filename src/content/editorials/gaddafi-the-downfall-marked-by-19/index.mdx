<Lead>
The first public announcement of the Quran's mathematical code in Arabic was not made in Cairo, Mecca or Tucson. It was broadcast from Radio Tarabulus in Tripoli, in January 1974, from a state whose ruler would later invite Dr. Rashad Khalifa to speak, have him arrested at the airport, and be told he was to be killed for heretical belief. Thirty-seven years after that broadcast, a coalition of nineteen nations began an intervention against Libya on 19 March 2011, under a resolution adopted at the Security Council's 6498th meeting.
</Lead>

This editorial assembles what the surviving record actually establishes about the relationship between Muammar Gaddafi's Libya and the messenger who first proclaimed Code 19 from a Libyan transmitter, and then audits the 19-based intervals in the collapse of 2011 rather than simply asserting them.

---

## 1. Tripoli, January 1974

Edip Yuksel holds an audio cassette that was in Khalifa's possession until his assassination in 1990. It carries Khalifa's own handwriting in permanent black marker: the year 1974, and in Arabic *I'jaz al-Qur'an, Radio Tarabulus*.<Ref id="1" />

<PullQuote attribution="Edip Yuksel, 19.org, January 2017">
In January 1974, Dr. Rashad Khalifa made one of the greatest discoveries in human history [...] Code 19 hidden in chapter 74 of the Quran, 19×74 lunar years after its revelation, and it is announced in Arabic through this radio station in Libya.
</PullQuote>

The tape sat unheard for forty-three years. Whatever else follows about Libya, the record begins with this: the state that would later jail him gave him his first Arabic-language platform.

## 2. The Science Adviser

Khalifa's connection to Libya was professional before it was religious. Published biography places him as science adviser to the Libyan government during 1975 and 1976, work that ran alongside his role as a technical expert for the United Nations Industrial Development Organization.<Ref id="2" /> The family lived in Tripoli; his son Sam attended the Oil Companies School there before the family settled in Tucson.

## 3. The Invitation and the Arrest

The clearest documentary account of what came later is not in a Submitters publication at all. It is in a teletype sent from FBI Los Angeles to the Director and to FBI Phoenix on 5 February 1990, five days after Khalifa was murdered in the Tucson mosque. The teletype was classified SECRET and released under FOIA with the classification struck through and the source names withheld under exemptions b6 and b7C.<Ref id="3" />

<Figure

  alt="Page three of an FBI teletype, the word SECRET struck through, recording that Rashad was invited to Libya approximately seven years earlier by Muommar Kaddaffi to present his controversial views in a speech, was promptly arrested and jailed by Libyan security agents on landing, advised he would be killed for his heretical religious beliefs, and subsequently released with a warning"

  height={1750}
  caption="FBI Los Angeles teletype, 5 February 1990, page three. Names of sources are withheld under FOIA exemptions b6 and b7C."

<PullQuote attribution="FBI teletype FBILA 0005, 5 February 1990, page three">
RASHAD WAS INVITED TO LIBYA APPROXIMATELY SEVEN YEARS AGO BY MUOMMAR KADDAFFI, IN ORDER TO PRESENT HIS CONTROVERSIAL VIEWS IN A SPEECH THERE. HOWEVER, HE WAS PROMPTLY ARRESTED AND JAILED BY LIBYAN SECURITY AGENTS WHEN HE LANDED IN THE COUNTRY AND ADVISED THAT HE WOULD BE KILLED BECAUSE OF HIS HERETICAL RELIGIOUS BELIEFS. HE WAS SUBSEQUENTLY RELEASED WITH A WARNING.
</PullQuote>

"Approximately seven years ago," measured from February 1990, places the invitation and the arrest around 1983. That dating matters: it puts the episode well after Khalifa had published the mathematical code and moved to the Quran-alone position, not during the adviser years of the mid-1970s. The same page records that Saudi consular officials separately visited him after he criticised the public execution of a Saudi princess, and offered him money to moderate his views.

The archive holds one further contemporaneous account, given to the *Arizona Daily Star* the day after the murder by Edip Yuksel, who worked as Khalifa's assistant:

<PullQuote attribution="Edip Yuksel, quoted in the Arizona Daily Star, 1 February 1990">
In 1976, he was a science adviser to Gadhafi, and later was imprisoned by the Libyan leader. [...] Some of Khalifa's friends were executed and [...] Khalifa was released from prison.
</PullQuote>

Crucially, the archive preserves Dr. Khalifa's own spoken testimony, recorded during a Friday sermon in Tucson on 26 November 1982, describing the exact circumstances of his detention:<Ref id="4" />

<PullQuote attribution="Dr. Rashad Khalifa, Friday Sermon, 26 November 1982">
When I got in trouble in Libya, and they threw me in the dungeon, I was very upset [...] for a couple of days. "Why is this happening to me?" And then, they don't give you any books, nothing. Not even clothes. I was afraid I will forget the Quran, so I was reading it continuously. [...] On the walls of the dungeon, I saw those lines marking days and weeks [...] I thought I'd never see my children again. And it happened before—they took Imam Musa Sadr of Lebanon, and he totally disappeared, never showed up again. [...] And then I came to a verse in Sura 2: "We belong to God, and to Him is our return." I totally submitted [...] Two days later, the President's men came in, kicked the doors, beat up the jailers, and had me in a government limousine going to the People's Palace. Ahmed, the bodyguard of Gaddafi, gave me a big hug and asked me what happened, when he is the one who put me in to start with.
</PullQuote>

## 4. What the Record Establishes and Does Not Settle

Three primary accounts of the detention survive: the declassified FBI teletype (which states an arrest at the airport followed by death threats for religious heresy), Edip Yuksel's 1990 statement to the press (which notes that friends were executed), and Khalifa's own 1982 sermon (which details the dungeon, the lack of due process, the tally marks of past victims, and the intervention of Gaddafi's bodyguard Ahmed).

The FBI file captures the theological motive directly: Khalifa was threatened with execution specifically because of his religious teachings—an exact parallel to the Quranic account of believers in Pharaoh's court:

<Verse chapter={40} verses="28">
A believing man among Pharaoh's people, who was concealing his belief, said, "How can you kill a man just for saying, 'My Lord is GOD,' and he has shown you clear proofs from your Lord? If he is a liar, that is his problem, and if he is truthful, you benefit from his promises. Surely, GOD does not guide any transgressor, liar."
</Verse>

Second, an earlier FBI communication cuts in an entirely different direction. A teletype from the Director dated 21 April 1980 states that Khalifa "is under investigation by FBI Houston for his alleged activity in connection with proposed Libyan terrorist actions in the United States."<Ref id="5" /> Nothing in the released file shows that allegation being sustained, and the 1990 file that followed his murder records that Los Angeles indices held no records identifiable with the victim and that he had no affiliation with religious or political organisations. The archive preserves the 1980 teletype because a reader consulting the file will find it, not because it establishes anything.

## 5. The Message That Was Delivered

Whatever happened at the airport, a message was demonstrably delivered to the Libyan state in writing, on a dated occasion, with a stated consequence.

The United Islamic Nation was declared in the *Muslim Perspective* of Muharram 1, 1406, that is 15 September 1985. Letters then went to the heads of state of twenty-seven countries. Libya is named among them.

## 6. The Collapse

The chronology of 2011 is a matter of public record and is set out here in full, because the arithmetic in the next section depends on it being stated before any of it is counted.

The uprising began in Benghazi on 17 February 2011.

The Security Council adopted **Resolution 1970** at its 6491st meeting on 26 February, imposing an arms embargo, freezing the Gaddafi family's overseas assets, and referring the Libyan leadership to the International Criminal Court.<Ref id="6" /> It adopted **Resolution 1973** at its 6498th meeting on 17 March, by ten votes to none with five abstentions, banning all flights in Libyan airspace and authorising "all necessary measures" to protect civilians.<Ref id="7" />

Coalition air operations opened on **19 March** as the American-led Operation Odyssey Dawn. Transfer of Authority to NATO took place on 30 March, and the first NATO air tasking order ran from 0600Z on 31 March, at which point the campaign became Operation Unified Protector.<Ref id="8" />

On 27 June the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants for Gaddafi, his son Saif al-Islam and his intelligence chief. Three days earlier, on **24 June**, the United States House of Representatives had voted on the intervention twice and authorised nothing: H.J.Res. 68, which would have authorised the limited use of American forces in support of the NATO mission, failed 123 to 295, and H.R. 2278, which would have cut off funds for it, failed 180 to 238. Both were brought to the floor under H.Res. 328.<Ref id="9" /> The campaign continued regardless.

The Royal Air Force's official history marks **19 August** as the turning point: "By 19 August, regime resistance was collapsing, and the FLF were converging on Tripoli from west and east."<Ref id="8" /> Tripoli fell within the week.

On **19 October**, a counterattack confined the last loyalist forces to District 2 of Sirte; on the morning of 20 October the remaining convoy attempted to break out, and Gaddafi was captured in a drainage culvert and killed.<Ref id="10" /> The National Transitional Council declared the country liberated on 23 October. Operation Unified Protector ended on **31 October**.

## 7. The Intervals

The observations fall into two classes, and the distinction between them matters more than the count of either.

### Fixed numbers

These are not intervals. Nobody chose the endpoints, because there are no endpoints to choose. Each is a serial number or a headcount that the institution itself fixed and published.

1. **The 6498th meeting.** Resolution 1973, the instrument that authorised force against Libya, was adopted at the Security Council's 6498th meeting. The Council has numbered its meetings continuously since 1946. 6498 = **19 × 342**, and because 342 = **19 × 18**, the meeting number is a double multiple of nineteen: **6498 = 19 × 19 × 18 = 19² × 18**. Resolution 1970, three weeks earlier, was the 6491st meeting, which is not a multiple of nineteen; the multiple falls on the meeting that authorised the use of force, not on the one that imposed sanctions.

2. **Nineteen nations.** The coalition comprised fifteen NATO members (Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Denmark, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Romania, Spain, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States) and four non-NATO participants (Jordan, Qatar, Sweden and the United Arab Emirates). **Nineteen** in total.<Ref id="11" />

3. **Four hundred and eighteen votes.** On 24 June 2011 the House of Representatives divided twice on Libya. On H.J.Res. 68, 123 members voted aye and 295 no; on H.R. 2278, 180 aye and 238 no. Both divisions were 418 members voting, none present, thirteen not voting. 418 = **19 × 22**. The second of the two is Roll Call **494 = 19 × 26**.<Ref id="9" />

### Dated intervals

These do depend on chosen endpoints, and are weaker for it. Each is given with its counting convention so a reader can reproduce or refute it.

4. **The triad of nineteenths.** Three decisive milestones of the 2011 campaign fell on the nineteenth of the month: **19 March** (opening of coalition air operations), **19 August** (the RAF-recorded collapse of regime resistance in Tripoli), and **19 October** (the final sealing of loyalist forces in Sirte before Gaddafi's capture).

5. **Resolution 1970 to Resolution 1973: 19 days.** 26 February to 17 March 2011 is exactly nineteen days, embargo to authorisation of force.

6. **Resolution 1973 to the end of the mission: 19 × 12.** 17 March to 31 October 2011 is 228 days. Counting instead from Resolution 1970 gives 247 days, or 19 × 13, which follows arithmetically from the two facts above rather than standing on its own.

7. **The whole of the rule: 19 × 810.** Gaddafi seized power on 1 September 1969 and was killed on 20 October 2011. Counting both endpoints, that span is 15,390 days, and 15,390 = **19 × 810**. Counted exclusively it is 15,389.

## 8. On Reading Intervals

The archive owes its readers a caution about the second half of the section above, because the honesty of the method is what gives the underlying miracle its force.

Any single date pair has roughly a one in nineteen chance of yielding a multiple of nineteen, and a chronology this dense offers a great many candidate pairs. Seventeen anchor dates were tested here, which is 136 pairs, and each was tested under both counting conventions: 272 trials. Eleven produced multiples of nineteen, where chance alone predicts about fourteen. The interval findings are therefore running at or slightly below the rate you would expect from any chronology whatever, and two of the eleven were discarded outright because they depended on dating the Radio Tripoli broadcast to a specific day when the archive knows only the month.

Dated intervals are consequently offered as a record of what the dates do, not as proof of anything. A reader who assembles a different set of anchors will find a comparable number of hits in the history of any war.

The fixed numbers are a different kind of evidence, and should not be lumped in with the intervals. The Security Council did not choose 6498 to mean anything; it is the running count of every meeting the Council has held since January 1946, and the meeting that authorised force against Libya happens to be 19² × 18. Nobody selected 418 either; it is simply how many representatives were on the floor. There are no endpoints to shop for in a serial number.

This is the same property that gives the Quran's composition its weight. The number of chapters, the counts of the initials and the position of Sura 74 were fixed before anyone counted them, and nobody chose them to make the totals work. Dates in a war are picked from an open field by whoever tabulates them. The difference is not a small one, and an archive that blurs it does the evidence no favours.

Among the intervals, item 7 is the strongest, because its endpoints are not selected at all: the first day of the regime and the last day of the man are the only two dates that span could possibly have.

## 9. The Preserved Record

The Libyan chapter of this archive is unusual in that almost none of it was produced by Submitters. The cassette is in Khalifa's handwriting but was published by Edip Yuksel. The account of the arrest is an FBI teletype declassified under FOIA. The account of the imprisonment is corroborated by Dr. Khalifa's own recorded voice from 1982 as well as contemporary press. The photograph of the handshake came to the archive without a date. The numbers in section 7 come from the United Nations, the Royal Air Force's own historians and the Clerk of the United States House of Representatives, none of whom had any interest in the question this editorial is asking. Only the warning of 1985 is a Submitters document, and it was printed and mailed six years before the man it warned had any reason to think it mattered.

Preserving the whole of that, including the 1980 allegation and the full account of the detention, is what makes the rest of it worth anything.
