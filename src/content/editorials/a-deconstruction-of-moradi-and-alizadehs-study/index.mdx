## The Fallacy of Isolated Sub-Testing

The primary flaw in Moradi and Alizadeh's methodology is their failure to account for **interlocking systems**. In statistical analysis, testing whether a single letter count is divisible by 19 in a random text yields a trivial probability of 1/19 (~5.26%). However, the Quranic miracle is not a single isolated count; it is a nested matrix where multiple independent counts hold simultaneously:

1. The total number of chapters: $114 = 19 \times 6$.
2. The number of letters in the opening *Basmalah*: $19 = 19 \times 1$.
3. The occurrences of each individual word of the *Basmalah* throughout the text (e.g., *Ism* 19×, *Allah* 2698×, *Al-Rahman* 57×, *Al-Rahim* 114×).
4. The letter distributions across all 29 initialed surahs conforming to exact multiples of 19.

## The Problem of Arbitrary Control Texts

To "prove" that any text can yield numerical patterns, the authors generated synthetic corpora with arbitrary search rules. This is the definition of **post-hoc data dredging** (the Texas sharpshooter fallacy). In the Quran, the parameters are pre-defined by the author through the presence of unique physical Quranic initials (e.g., *A.L.M.*, *Qaf*, *Ya-Sin*), placed explicitly at the start of specific chapters over 1,400 years ago.

## Conclusion: Empirical Rigor Wins

When the complete, audited text is evaluated under standard information theory and combinatorial probability models, the conclusions of Moradi and Alizadeh fall apart.
