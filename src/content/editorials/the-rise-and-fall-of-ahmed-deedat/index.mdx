<Lead>
Ahmed Deedat signed the acknowledgment page of *Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle* on 19 February 1979. In it he named his source without hedging: "that great servant of Islam — Dr. Rashad Khalifa Ph.D." The booklet carried the 19-based mathematical structure of the Quran out of Tucson and into Durban, Lagos, London and the lecture circuit. Nine years later Deedat sent Khalifa a telegram addressing him as *al-kazzaab*, the liar, and withdrew the booklet, the tapes and the videos from circulation.
</Lead>

This editorial sets the surviving record side by side: the booklet itself, the full-length lecture recording, the Masjid Tucson bulletins that sold Deedat's work, the telegram exchange of 21 May 1988, and the filmed question-and-answer session in which Deedat retracted. Where Deedat's account and Khalifa's account of the same events disagree, both are reproduced.

---

## 1. The Booklet

*Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle* runs to seventy-five printed pages across nine chapters, published by the Islamic Propagation Centre International in Durban.<Ref id="1" /> The cover blurb states the thesis in the language of proof rather than devotion:

<PullQuote attribution="Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle, cover">
Using the ultimate in scientific proof, namely, Mathematics, Mr. Deedat discusses herein Physical, Examinable evidence that the QUR'AN is the Infallible Word of GOD.
</PullQuote>

The acknowledgment is unambiguous about authorship of the findings:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, acknowledgment, 19/2/79">
In the service of AL-QUR'AN-UL-KAREEM, I have in all humility taken liberty of bringing the scientific findings of that great servant of Islam — Dr. Rashad Khalifa Ph.D. in my own humble way. [...] In it I have freely utilised Dr. Khalifa's data and at places I have used his actual eloquent words verbatim for lack of better expression.
</PullQuote>

The chapter sequence moves from apologetics into arithmetic: *Background to the Beginning*, *Scientific Proof of Qur'anic Revelations*, *The Purity of the Holy Book*, *Authentication from the Giants of Literature*, *"Over It Are Nineteen"*, *Calculations and the Centuries*, *There Was No Human Author*, *The Mathematical Miracle*, and *Prophecy and Fulfilment*. Chapter 6 confronts the count of 113 *basmalas* against 114 suras and works through Sura 9 to resolve it. Chapter 8 tabulates the Quranic initials, including the 26,676 occurrences of *alif*, *lām* and *mīm* across the *Alif Lām Mīm* suras, a total Deedat notes is 19 × 1404.

Deedat returns the credit again on page 66, after the last of the diagrams:

<PullQuote attribution="Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle, pp. 66–67">
In this treatise I have barely touched the tip of the "iceberg" of this phenomenal discovery. To those who wish to delve deeper into this subject. I heartily recommend a booklet and tape by Dr. Rashad Khalifa, Ph.D. [...] I am personally indebted to Dr. Khalifa for opening my eyes on this subject. May Allah grant him long life to selflessly serve the Cause of Islam.
</PullQuote>

The closing chapter cites Khalifa's *The Perpetual Miracle of Muhammad* for the computed odds against coincidence, which Deedat prints in full as 626,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 to 1, and then sets out five effects he expects the findings to produce, ending with the expectation that they "will expose the bigots and the hypocrites as unfortunate fanatics."

## 2. The Lecture

The archive holds a full-length recording of Deedat delivering the material live, running one hour and forty-five minutes.<Ref id="2" /> It opens on the argument he built the whole talk around: that if a man composed this book, that man would have to be God.

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, lecture recording, 00:00">
If he did this job what I'm going to show you now, visually what you see. If he did it, you'll have to start worshipping him. And he says, don't worship me, worship God. The only way out for this, for the skeptic, the unbeliever, the enemy is to say, it's a coincidence.
</PullQuote>

Reaching 74:30, Deedat does something the later retraction makes worth recording precisely. He reviews the two classical readings of *"over it are nineteen"* and rejects both. Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī had read the nineteen as human faculties; Ashraf Ali Thanwi, whom Deedat introduces as a *mujaddid* of the subcontinent and a father of Darul Uloom Deoband, had read them as nineteen fundamentals of the religion. Deedat sets both aside:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, lecture recording, 24:43">
These are all tafsirs. I say, it needs no tafsir. The answer is in the mouth of your little ones [...] Ask your son or your daughter, what is 19? And your child will tell you, daddy, it's 10 + 9. Am I right? [...] 19 means 19.
</PullQuote>

He then walks the audience through the visual case: Sura 96 standing nineteenth from the end of the Quran, its nineteen verses, the nineteen words of the first revelation, their seventy-six letters (19 × 4), the 114 chapters (19 × 6). Each is followed by the same rhetorical beat, the skeptic answering "coincidence," which Deedat calls bleating like a goat. He is explicit that no interpretation is being offered at all:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, lecture recording, 34:13">
This is something visual. You can see with your own eyes. I'm not giving you any interpretation. No, no meaning. I'm giving you no meaning. I just look at this pattern. Can any man or group of human beings go and write a book like this?
</PullQuote>

Two features of the recording bear noting. Deedat presents the findings throughout without naming Rashad Khalifa on the recording, although the booklet he is selling from the stage names him three times. And he tells the audience the booklet has sold out, with more due by May.

## 3. Distribution on Both Sides

The relationship was not one-directional. Masjid Tucson, Khalifa's own organisation, sold Deedat's booklet through its catalogue in *Muslim Perspective*, listed fifth among its titles and shelved between Khalifa's own works:

<PullQuote attribution="Muslim Perspective, December 1985, p. 4">
5. Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle. by Ahmad Deedat. Simplified presentation of the Miracle with detailed illustrations . . . $3.80
</PullQuote>

Its reach ran further than either organisation's own mailing list. The July 1985 issue prints a letter from Yusuf K. Abiodum of Ado-Ekiti, Nigeria, who had been shaken in a doctrinal argument and wrote to Tucson only because a locally published edition of the Deedat booklet had reached him:

<PullQuote attribution="Yusuf K. Abiodum, Nigeria, printed in Muslim Perspective, July 1985">
My happiness was restored when I read a book called AL-QURAN THE ULTIMATE MIRACLE by Ahmad Deedat summarizing your discovery of the Mathematical Miracle of Quran, and published by the Islamic Education Trust of Nigeria.
</PullQuote>

By the mid-1980s the booklet was circulating in at least three imprints across two continents, was stocked by the discoverer himself, and was functioning as the entry point through which readers found Khalifa's underlying research.

## 4. The Telegram, 21 May 1988

The break is dated by a Western Union transmission. On 21 May 1988 Deedat wired Khalifa in Tucson:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, telegram to Rashad Khalifa, 21 May 1988">
TO RASHAD KHALIFA AL KAZZAAB. I BESEECH YOU TO RENOUNCE YOUR FALSE CLAIMS TO MESSENGERSHIP OF GOD AND ACCEPT MUHUMMED IBNU ABDILLAH AS THE FINAL PROPHET (NABEE) AND MESSENGER (RASOOL) OF ALLAH. IF YOU FAIL TO DO THIS THEN I CHALLENGE YOU TO AN OPEN DEBATE TO DISPROVE ALL YOUR FALSE CLAIMS AT THE MADISON SQUARE GARDEN NEW YORK - THE CENTRE OF ACTIVITIES IN THE USA WE WILL PAY ALL THE COSTS INCURRED. AHMED DEEDAT (SERVANT OF ISLAM)
</PullQuote>

Khalifa published the exchange in the October 1988 issue with the confirmation copy of his own reply, timed the same afternoon:

<PullQuote attribution="Rashad Khalifa, reply telegram, 21 May 1988, 0359P EST">
TO AHMAD DEEDAT, SERVANT OF SHAYTAN, I ACCEPT YOUR CHALLENGE. I NAME THE PLACE, RASHAD KHALIFA
</PullQuote>

The two men's accounts of what followed do not agree, and the archive preserves both. Khalifa's charge, printed under the headline *DEEDAT: A PROVEN KAZZAAB*, is that Deedat afterwards told audiences the challenge had gone unanswered, and that the Western Union confirmation copy disproves it.<Ref id="3" /> Deedat's account, given later from the platform, is that Khalifa declined the venue and asked for a private meeting in Tucson instead:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, filmed question-and-answer session, 01:54">
So, he says, "No, I don't want to come to the Madison Square Garden. You come to Tucson" [...] in the private he wants to have a discussion.
</PullQuote>

What is not in dispute is that the debate never took place.

## 5. "Kazzaab"

Khalifa's response to the telegram was not primarily about the debate. It was about a single word. The telegram is composed entirely in English apart from one transliterated Arabic term, and that term is the one the Quran places in the mouths of those who reject a messenger. The October 1988 front page carries the argument as its banner:

<PullQuote attribution="Muslim Perspective, October 1988, p. 1">
THEY WILL FIND OUT TOMORROW WHO THE 'KAZZAAB' IS! [54:25]. Incredible Quranic prophecy has forced Ahmed Deedat to use the same word predicted by the Quran to describe God's messenger. Deedat's telegram is all in English, except for the word "KAZZAAB"!!!
</PullQuote>

<Verse chapter={54} verses="24-26">
They said, "Shall we follow one of us; a human being? We will then go astray, then end up in Hell. Did the message come down to him, instead of us? He is a flagrant liar." They will find out tomorrow who the flagrant liar is.
</Verse>

The word rendered "liar" in both places is *kadhdhāb*. The same word appears in the same accusation at 38:4, where the disbelievers answer a warner sent from among themselves with "A magician; a liar."

Khalifa made the same point twice from the floor, and the archive holds both recordings. At the Third International Conference of the United Islamic Nation in 1988 he held up the telegram itself:

<PullQuote attribution="Rashad Khalifa, UIN International Conference, 1988, 15:33">
Here I get this telegram from Ahmad Deedat addressed to Rashad Khalifa, AL-KAZZAAB. This is in English, but he writes the Arabic word that is in the Quran. [...] I mean this is Western Union, folks. And they wrote down K A Z Z A B. It's not an English word. But it's exactly what God wrote in the Quran in Sura 38 and in Sura 54.
</PullQuote>

In the same session Ismail Barakat notes the one difference between the telegram and the scripture: the Quranic word is *kadhdhāb*, spelled with *dhāl*, and the telegram spells it with *zāy*.<Ref id="4" />

## 6. The Retraction

The withdrawal is preserved on film. Asked from the floor whether he still endorsed *Al-Qur'an: The Ultimate Miracle*, Deedat gave the shortest answer in the archive.

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, filmed question-and-answer session, 02:16">
I've no more book, no more video, no more cassette, finish.
</PullQuote>

Three things in the surrounding answer bear on the record already set out above. First, Deedat now describes the discovery as one he had merely found useful: "this man who originally made the discovery, which I found useful in talking to Muslims and non-Muslims." Second, he introduces a qualification that appears nowhere in the booklet or the lecture, "There are certain flaws in the theory," and does not name one. Third, he gives his reason for withdrawal in terms of communal cost rather than evidence:

<PullQuote attribution="Ahmed Deedat, filmed question-and-answer session, 02:25">
There's so much more there, we don't want to create, you know, unnecessary strife among the Muslims [...] Discontinued, finish, no more.
</PullQuote>

The withdrawal covered the booklet, the audio cassettes and the video tapes together. Nothing in the archive shows Deedat afterwards addressing the arithmetic he had presented as visual, examinable and requiring no interpretation.

## 7. Surah 74

Surah 74, *Al-Muddath-thir*, carries the mathematical sign of the Quran at verse 30. The verses preceding it describe an individual endowed with wealth and reach, who examines the proof, calculates, and turns away calling it human speech.

<Verse chapter={74} verses="11-16">
Let Me deal with one I created as an individual. I provided him with lots of money. And children to behold. I made everything easy for him. Yet, he is greedy for more. He stubbornly refused to accept these proofs.
</Verse>

<Verse chapter={74} verses="18-26">
For he reflected, then decided. Miserable is what he decided. Miserable indeed is what he decided. He looked. He frowned and whined. Then he turned away arrogantly. He said, "This is but clever magic! This is human made." I will commit him to retribution.
</Verse>

The sequence in the record follows the sequence in the passage: exposure to the proof, promotion of it, calculation, refusal, and the verdict that it is human work. Deedat's own lecture had already stated the stakes of that last step. He told his audience that the skeptic who attributes the pattern to Muhammad would, if consistent, have to worship him, and that the only escape left was to call it coincidence.

## 8. The Interval

The archive preserves one further calculation, made on the dates themselves. From the telegram of 21 May 1988 to Deedat's death on 8 August 2005 is 6,289 days counting both endpoints, which is 19 × 331.<Ref id="5" />

In May 1996, following a speaking tour in Australia, Deedat suffered a stroke that left him paralysed and unable to speak or swallow. He remained conscious and bedridden, communicating by eye movement, for the remaining nine years of his life.<Ref id="6" />

<Verse chapter={74} verses="30-31">
Over it is nineteen. We appointed angels to be guardians of Hell, and we assigned their number (19) (1) to disturb the disbelievers, (2) to convince the Christians and Jews (that this is a divine scripture), (3) to strengthen the faith of the faithful, (4) to remove all traces of doubt from the hearts of Christians, Jews, as well as the believers, and (5) to expose those who harbor doubt in their hearts, and the disbelievers.
</Verse>

## 9. The Lesson for the Archive

The record here is unusual in one respect: almost every stage of it was published by the participants themselves. Deedat printed his own acknowledgment of Khalifa, filmed his own lecture, and sat for the question in which he withdrew all of it. Khalifa printed the telegram he received and the confirmation copy of the reply he sent. The archive adds nothing to either side beyond keeping them in one place and in order.

That is the value of holding primary sources rather than accounts of them. A man's own words, dated and preserved, are the only witness that cannot be revised after the fact.
