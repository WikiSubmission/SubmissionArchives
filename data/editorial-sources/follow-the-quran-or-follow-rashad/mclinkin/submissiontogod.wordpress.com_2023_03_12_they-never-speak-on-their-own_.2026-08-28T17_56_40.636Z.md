\[Quran 21:24\] Have they found other gods beside Him? Say, “Show me your proof. This is the message to my generation, consummating all previous messages.” Indeed, most of them do not recognize the truth; this is why they are so hostile.

\[Quran 21:25\] We did not send any messenger before you except with the inspiration: “There is no god except Me; you shall worship Me alone.”

\[Quran 21:26\] Yet, they said, “The Most Gracious has begotten a son!” Glory be to Him. All (messengers) are (His) honored servants.

\[Quran 21:27\] **They never speak on their own, and they strictly follow His commands.**

\[Quran 21:28\] He knows their future and their past. They do not **intercede**, except for those already accepted by Him, and they are worried about their own necks.

\[Quran 21:29\] If any of them claims to be a god beside Him, we requite him with Hell; we thus requite the wicked.

Some say this verse in 21:27 is talking about Angels, which is fine and it can fit the interpretation in the context of that chapter, however, since the discussion of Jesus came right before, it can reasonably be referring to messengers. And of course, it could even be referring to both, as in all creations (including angels and messengers) never speak on their own and strictly follow God’s commands.

The main thing I want to discuss here is that this verse is often misused to mean “messengers don’t have free will, they can only say what God tells them to say, and always obey God’s commands”. However, this verse _cannot_ mean that messengers can never say things from their own shortcomings or that messengers can never stray if it is in accordance with God’s will. Jonah did not strictly follow God’s commands and was punished for it. And so was Muhammad who made the mistake of not obeying God’s commands and he was called out for it (ie. his marriage to Zeid’s former wife). Muhammad even gave a false command by telling Zaid to “keep his wife and reverence God” (see verse: 33:37). Muhammad literally said something, **spoke** something, that is against what God had commanded him. And how David spoke on his own when he judged between 2 people and repented for it. And how Abraham spoke on his own when he prayed to God to forgive his idol-worshiping father and how God told him this was not an appropriate prayer for Abraham to make. So messengers can indeed say things from their own shortcomings (And verse 34:50 explicitly says this).

So what does 21:27 mean? Reading it in context, it becomes very clear this verse is about **_intercession_**. The verse immediately after even goes into intercession and shuts down any idea that anyone can intercede. Rather than a metaphorical interpretation that this verse is saying messengers don’t say things unless God inspired them to say it, this is actually literal: messengers _literally_ cannot speak independently; they cannot intercede unless God lets them. So anyone claiming Jesus is God’s son (21:26), is in essence claiming there is this powerful deity’s son who has the power to speak on his own and advocate for you in the presence of God. But God shuts down this thought in this verse–and He even goes to the length of saying that all of his messengers aren’t even capable of speaking unless God permits them-so how are they gonna intercede for you in any independent capacity? Your reckoning is completely controlled by God. The messengers’ own capability to speak itself is under the control of God. The function of the muscles of the vocal cord and mouth itself is under the control of God. Nothing can speak on their own; no one can speak independently; no one can speak unless God permits it. No one can intercede for you unless God permits it, and God only accepts the truthful testimony, and if God knows what the truth is, then what can anyone say that God doesn’t already know? Thus, there is no effective intercession.

The Arabic of that verse further supports this. The Arabic reads “they do not precede him in speech”–basically meaning: they do not speak before God speaks; they do not speak unless spoken to.

This verse read in context serves as a shutdown of any concept of intercession or messengers doing anything independently of God, including even speaking. All messengers are God’s servants, and they are not allowed to speak unless God lets them and they are required to strictly follow his commands. This is a Master-servant relationship. You have no effective intercessors; you only have one advocate: God.
