# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Submission Archives serves three overlapping audiences:

- Members of the Submission community returning to familiar recordings, writings, newsletters, and Qur'an material.
- Researchers seeking exact passages, timestamps, pages, source context, and stable citations.
- Newcomers encountering Dr. Rashad Khalifa's work and the Submission archive for the first time.

Visitors may arrive to browse historically, follow a specific topic, verify a quotation, read a document, or continue into a complete recording. The experience must welcome unfamiliar visitors without reducing the depth required by experienced researchers.

## Product Purpose

Submission Archives preserves and presents the work of Dr. Rashad Khalifa as an enduring digital archive. It should feel like an editorial museum experience with powerful search, not like a generic media platform or database frontend.

Success means visitors can understand what the archive contains, discover meaningful material, move from an excerpt to its full source, and verify what they cite. Presentation should honor the historical material while making a large mixed-media corpus legible and usable on contemporary devices.

## Brand Personality

Scholarly, reverent, enduring.

The experience should feel calm, considered, trustworthy, and archival. Its voice is confident without being promotional, welcoming without becoming casual, and visually distinctive without competing with the source material.

## Anti-references

- Flashy SaaS landing pages with inflated claims, abstract product decoration, or dashboard aesthetics.
- Glass-heavy interfaces where blur, transparency, and layered cards become the visual identity.
- Generic streaming services that treat every recording as interchangeable entertainment.
- Decorative animation that delays reading, searching, or reaching the original source.
- Visual redesigns that replace the established Submission Archives logo or abandon the current warm cream, ink, and rust color identity.

## Design Principles

1. **Treat every item as an artifact.** Titles, dates, speakers, editions, page numbers, and source relationships provide historical context and should remain legible.
2. **Let the archive lead.** Photography, documents, recordings, and quotations carry the experience. Interface decoration supports them quietly.
3. **Connect discovery to evidence.** Search excerpts should always lead clearly to the exact timestamp, page, verse, or complete source.
4. **Welcome first visits, reward deep research.** Plain orientation and recognizable pathways should coexist with precise filters and dense source detail.
5. **Preserve identity while improving restraint.** Keep the Submission Archives logo and established palette, reduce glass effects, and create distinction through typography, rhythm, material framing, and editorial composition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across core routes and interactions. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus states, semantic structure, adequate contrast in both themes, text zoom resilience, touch targets appropriate for mobile, and complete reduced-motion behavior. Arabic and right-to-left Qur'an content must retain correct reading direction and appropriate typography. The interface should remain understandable to visitors who do not already know the archive's terminology.
